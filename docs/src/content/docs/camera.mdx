<Api of="MapController" /> reads and moves the camera from anywhere, as a
`Scroller` does for a list. Create one, pass it to one map, and keep it; when
the map leaves the screen and comes back, the new map starts where the old one
left the camera. Its getters are observable, so a component that reads them
updates when they change.

## Where the camera is

<Api of="MapController.camera" display="camera" /> is where the camera is, and
changes with every frame of a move or a gesture. It's always there: before a map
shows the controller, it's the camera that the controller starts with. The
map's `onCameraIdle` tells you where the camera comes to rest.
<Api of="MapController.viewport" display="viewport" /> is the latest frame that
the map drew, a <Api of="Viewport" />, with the same camera:

<Code code={region(examples, "camera-observable")} lang="ts" />

A viewport also converts between the frame's screen points and coordinates, as
the library's controls and markers do. It changes every frame, so read it in
`@Computed` getters of the components that need it.

## While the camera moves

<Api of="MapController.cameraMoving" display="cameraMoving" /> is true while
the camera moves, from the map's `onCameraMoveStart` until its `onCameraIdle`,
and <Api of="MapController.cameraMoveReason" display="cameraMoveReason" />
says what moved it last: `CameraMoveReason.GESTURE` for the user's input on
the map, and `CameraMoveReason.PROGRAMMATIC` for the app, such as a move, a
control, or following the user's location. Both are observable, so a panel can
step aside while the user moves the map, and stay while the app moves it:

<Code code={region(examples, "camera-moving")} lang="ts" />

## Move the camera

<Api of="MapController.updateCamera" display="updateCamera()" /> changes the
fields of the camera that you give it, and its options choose whether it jumps,
eases, or flies (<Api of="CameraTransitionMode" />):

<Code code={region(examples, "camera-move")} lang="ts" />

`fitCameraToBounds()`, `fitCameraToCoordinates()`, and `fitCameraToGeometry()`
fit an area, coordinates, or a GeoJSON geometry. Each move resolves `true` when
the camera got there, and `false` when something stopped it first:

<Code code={region(examples, "camera-fit")} lang="ts" />

A move that the app makes before a map shows the controller waits: the map
makes it once it shows its first frame at its size, so a fit fits the map's real
size. Moves wait while no map shows the controller, and go in the order the app
made them; `cancelCameraTransitions()` stops them.

## Padding

The camera's `padding` is space, in vp, around the part of the map that it
centers in. `updateCamera()` sets it with the rest of the camera, and it stays
until a move or a fit changes it:

<Code code={region(examples, "camera-padding")} lang="ts" />

A fit's `fitPadding` is a margin around what it fits, for that fit alone. Its
`cameraPadding` sets the padding that the camera keeps after the fit; without
it, the camera keeps its own. See <Api of="FitCameraOptions" />.

## Keep the camera clear of your UI

When your UI covers part of the map, such as a bottom panel, pass the edges it
covers to the map's `viewportInsets`. The control presets, such as
<Api of="DefaultControls" />, keep their controls
clear of them, and each move and fit centers in the part of the map that the
user sees:

<Code code={region(examples, "viewport-insets")} lang="ts" />

The insets add to the camera's padding on the map, but the controller leaves
them out of every camera it reports. So the camera you read or save doesn't
change with the panel, and a saved camera restores the same way under another
panel.

## Save the camera

The controller keeps the camera while the app runs. The system can end the
app's process in the background, and start it again when the user returns. To
bring the camera back then, save it in the ability's `onSaveState()`, and start
the controller from it: a <Api of="CameraPosition" /> survives JSON as it is.
Here the page keeps its controller in `AppStorageV2`, where the ability can reach
it:

<Code code={region(examples, "save-camera")} lang="ts" />

The system keeps that state only while the app is in the background. To reopen
where the user left the camera after they close the app, keep the saved camera
in `PersistenceV2` instead.

## What the library doesn't cover

<Api of="MapController.withFfiMap" display="withFfiMap()" /> lends the FFI map
and its render session to a callback, for what the library doesn't cover yet.
