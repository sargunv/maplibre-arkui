<Api of="MapController" /> reads and moves the camera from anywhere, as a
`Scroller` does for a list. Create one, pass it to one map, and keep it; when
the map leaves the screen and comes back, the new map starts where the old one
left the camera. `camera`, `styleLoaded`, `attributions`, and
`followingUserLocation` are observable, so a component that reads them updates
when they change:

<Code code={region(examples, "camera-observable")} lang="ts" />

`camera` changes when the camera comes to rest;
<Api of="MapController.getCamera" display="getCamera()" /> reads where it is
now. <Api of="MapController.updateCamera" display="updateCamera()" /> jumps,
eases, or flies; `fitCameraToLatLngBounds()`, `fitCameraToLatLngs()`, and
`fitCameraToGeometry()` fit an area, coordinates, or a GeoJSON geometry; and
each resolves with how the move ended:

## Padding

The camera's `padding` is space, in vp, around the part of the map that it
centers in. `updateCamera()` sets it with the rest of the camera, and it stays
until a move or a fit changes it:

<Code code={region(examples, "camera-padding")} lang="ts" />

A fit's `fitPadding` is a margin around what it fits, for that fit alone. Its
`cameraPadding` sets the padding that the camera keeps after the fit; without
it, the camera keeps its own. See <Api of="FitCameraOptions" />.

## Keep the camera clear of your UI

When your UI covers part of the map, such as a bottom panel, pass the edges it
covers to the map's `viewportInsets`. The ornaments stay clear of them, and
each move and fit centers in the part of the map that the user sees:

<Code code={region(examples, "viewport-insets")} lang="ts" />

The insets add to the camera's padding on the map, but the controller leaves
them out of every camera it reports. So the camera you read or save doesn't
change with the panel, and a saved camera restores the same way under another
panel.

## Save the camera

The controller keeps the camera while the app runs. The system can end the
app's process in the background, and start it again when the user returns. To
bring the camera back then, save it in the ability's `onSaveState()`, and start
the controller from it with `cameraToOptions()`. Here the page keeps its
controller in `AppStorageV2`, where the ability can reach it:

<Code code={region(examples, "save-camera")} lang="ts" />

The system keeps that state only while the app is in the background. To reopen
where the user left the camera after they close the app, keep the saved camera
in `PersistenceV2` instead.

## The frame on screen

`frameCamera`, `pixelForLatLng()`, `latLngForPixel()`, and
`metersPerPixelAtLatitude()` follow the frame on screen, and are observable too,
so content placed with them moves with the map. They change every frame, so read
them in `@Computed` getters of the components that need them. The controller
also exposes the FFI `runtime`, `map`, and `renderSession`, for what this
library doesn't wrap.
