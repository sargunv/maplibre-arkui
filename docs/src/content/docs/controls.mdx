<Api of="MaplibreMap" /> draws ArkUI content over the map from its trailing
closure, its overlay. Without a closure, it draws
<Api of="MapDefaultOverlay" />: a scale bar and a compass along the top edge,
and the MapLibre logo and an attribution button along the bottom edge. The scale
bar shows for a moment as the zoom changes, and the compass while the map is
rotated or tilted. The controls follow dark mode, and their labels and
accessibility text are in the app's language.

## Choose a preset

Put a preset in the closure to choose the controls.
<Api of="MapFullOverlay" /> adds zoom buttons to the default controls, for users
with a mouse and for accessibility. <Api of="MapAttributionOnlyOverlay" /> keeps
only the logo and the attribution button.

<Code code={region(examples, "overlay-preset")} lang="ts" />

An empty closure draws nothing. The sources' terms usually require their
attribution, so show the controller's `attributions` elsewhere:

<Code code={region(examples, "overlay-none")} lang="ts" />

The presets keep their controls clear of the map's `viewportInsets`, as
[the camera](/maplibre-arkui/camera/#keep-the-camera-clear-of-your-ui)
describes.

## Add your own controls

The closure lays out its children in a `Stack` the size of the map. Each control
is a component that finds its map wherever it is in the closure, so it needs no
controller: <Api of="MapCompassButton" />, <Api of="MapZoomButtons" />,
<Api of="MapScaleBar" />, <Api of="MapAttributionButton" />,
<Api of="MapLibreLogo" />, and the ones that show only while they matter,
<Api of="MapDisappearingCompassButton" /> and
<Api of="MapDisappearingScaleBar" />. Lay them out as you would any ArkUI
component, and let touches between them through to the map with
`hitTestBehavior(HitTestMode.Transparent)`:

## Pin content to a coordinate

<Api of="MapPlacedAt" /> puts its content on a coordinate and moves it with the
map. Its `alignment` picks the point of the content that sits on the
coordinate; `Alignment.Bottom` stands a label on it:

<Code code={region(examples, "placed-at")} lang="ts" />

## Point to a coordinate off the screen

<Api of="MapPointerPinButton" /> shows a pin on an oval inside the map that
points towards a coordinate off the screen, and hides while the coordinate is
inside the oval. Pad its parent to move the oval in from the map's edges:

<Code code={region(examples, "placed-towards")} lang="ts" />

To draw your own pointer, put it in <Api of="MapPlacedTowards" />, whose
`onAngleChange` gives the direction to turn it.
