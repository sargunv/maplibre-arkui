<Api of="MaplibreMap" /> draws ArkUI content over the map from its trailing
closure, its overlay. Without a closure, it draws <Api of="DefaultControls" />:
a scale bar and a compass along the top edge, and the MapLibre logo and an
attribution button along the bottom edge. The scale bar shows for a moment as
the zoom changes, and the compass while the map is rotated or tilted. The
controls follow dark mode, and their labels and accessibility text are in the
app's language. They come from the library's resources, whose names start with
`maplibre_`; those names aren't part of the API and can change.

## Choose a preset

Put a preset in the closure to choose the controls.
<Api of="FullControls" /> adds zoom buttons to the default controls, for users
with a mouse and for accessibility. <Api of="AttributionControls" /> keeps only
the logo and the attribution button.

An empty closure draws nothing. The sources' terms usually require their
attribution, so show the controller's `attributions` elsewhere. They're HTML,
which <Api of="AttributionText" /> shows as text with working links:

<Code code={region(examples, "overlay-none")} lang="ts" />

The presets keep their controls clear of the map's `viewportInsets`, as
[the camera](/maplibre-arkui/camera/#keep-the-camera-clear-of-your-ui)
describes.

## Add your own controls

The closure lays out its children in a `Stack` the size of the map. Each control
is a component that finds its map wherever it is in the closure, so it needs no
controller: <Api of="CompassButton" />, <Api of="ZoomButtons" />,
<Api of="ScaleBar" />, <Api of="AttributionButton" />,
<Api of="MaplibreLogo" />, and the ones that show only while they matter,
<Api of="DisappearingCompassButton" /> and <Api of="DisappearingScaleBar" />.
Lay them out as you would any ArkUI component, and let touches between them
through to the map with `hitTestBehavior(HitTestMode.Transparent)`:

<Code code={region(examples, "overlay-custom")} lang="ts" />

The controls, presets, and markers work only in the closure. ArkUI draws the
builders of `bindSheet`, `bindPopup`, and `bindContextMenu` outside the map,
even when they're attached to a component in the closure, so those don't count
as the overlay. Outside it, a control logs an error that names it and draws
nothing. The logo is the exception: it works anywhere.

## Pin content to a coordinate

<Api of="Marker" /> puts its content on a coordinate and moves it with the map.
Its `alignment` picks the point of the content that sits on the coordinate;
`Alignment.Bottom` stands a label on it. The marker is the size of its content,
so taps beside it reach the map. Chain attributes such as `.onClick()` on the
content rather than on the marker:

## Point to a coordinate off the screen

<Api of="PointerPinButton" /> shows a pin on an oval inside the map that points
towards a coordinate off the screen, and hides while the coordinate is inside
the oval. Its `label` is what screen readers read for it. The oval fills the
content box of its parent, so size the parent to the map and pad it to move the
oval in from the map's edges:

<Code code={region(examples, "offscreen-marker")} lang="ts" />

To draw your own pointer, put it in <Api of="OffscreenMarker" />, whose
`onAngleChange` gives the direction to turn it.
