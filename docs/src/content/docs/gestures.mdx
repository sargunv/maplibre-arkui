Users pan, zoom, rotate, and pitch the map by default:

- **Touch**: drag to pan, pinch to zoom, twist to rotate, and drag two fingers
  up or down to pitch. Double tap to zoom in, double tap and drag to zoom, and
  tap with two fingers to zoom out.
- **Mouse**: drag to pan, and drag with the right button to rotate (side to
  side) and pitch (up and down). The wheel zooms at the pointer.
- **Touchpad**: swipe two fingers to pan, and pinch to zoom.
- **Keys**, once a touch or a click focuses the map: hold the arrows to pan,
  Shift and the arrows to rotate and pitch, and plus and minus to zoom.

A drag that starts on the map moves the map, even in a `Scroll`. Content over
the map that handles its own gestures, such as a button, keeps them.

## Choose the camera's movements

`interactions` turns movements off, one at a time. This map pans and zooms but
stays north-up and flat, and tells the app while the user moves it:

<Code code={region(examples, "gestures")} lang="ts" />

`onGestureStart` and `onGestureEnd` bracket each gesture: any input that moves
the camera, with its momentum and a rotation's snap. The camera's move starts
with the map's `onCameraMoveStart` after `onGestureStart`, unless the camera was
moving already, and ends with its `onCameraIdle` after `onGestureEnd`. To react
to one movement,
give it an `onStart`, which runs each time a gesture starts to move the camera
that way. A pan already stops the camera from
[following the user's location](/maplibre-arkui/location/); an app that moves
the camera itself, such as to follow a vehicle, stops as the user starts to pan.

## Scroll the wheel and the touchpad

The wheel zooms at the pointer, and pans with Ctrl held. With `wheel` set to
`WheelAction.PAN`, it pans, and zooms with Ctrl. A two-finger swipe on a
touchpad pans either way, and zooms with Ctrl.

A map in a `Scroll` or `List` can leave the page's gestures to it: with
`cooperative`, one-finger drags, the wheel, and touchpad swipes scroll the page.
The map moves with two fingers. With Ctrl held, the wheel does what `wheel`
says, and a touchpad swipe pans.

## Momentum

A drag that lifts while it moves keeps the map gliding, and the glide slows the
way a `Scroll` or `List` slows after a fling. A pinch, twist, or tilt that lifts
while it moves glides briefly too. A touch, a wheel, a key, or a camera move by
the app stops the glide, and the gesture ends with `onGestureEnd` once the
camera stops.

## Snap and tick rotation

A rotation that ends within 7° of north eases to it after its glide, and its
gesture ends, with `onGestureEnd`, once the camera gets there. `snap` chooses
the bearings to snap to, every `spacing` degrees from `offset`, and how near one
the rotation has to end. `{ enabled: false }` turns snapping off.

As a twist or a right-button drag turns the map across north, the device plays
a soft tick. `haptics` chooses where it ticks, in the same way, and
`{ enabled: false }` turns the ticks off. Ticks follow the system's touch feedback setting, and they need the
vibrator permission, which the library can't declare for its apps. An app that
wants them declares it in its `module.json5`:

```json5
"requestPermissions": [{ "name": "ohos.permission.VIBRATE" }]
```

The system grants it at install, without asking the user. Without it, the map
plays no ticks and reports nothing.

## Clicks

A tap is a click, and a long press or a right click is a long click. The map's
`onMapClick` or `onMapLongClick` gets it first, and returns `true` to
consume it. Then the layers of the [content](/maplibre-arkui/content/) with features
under it get it, from the top layer down, until a layer's `onClick` or
`onLongClick` returns `true`; a handler that throws passes it on. A click that
nothing consumed goes to `onUnhandledClick`, but a long click doesn't:

A layer's handler gets a <Api of="FeatureClickEvent" />: the layer's features
under the click, front first, and the click's point and coordinate. `hitPadding`
widens what counts as under the click, so small circles and thin lines are
easier to tap:

While zoom is on, a tap waits 300 ms for a second tap, so a double tap zooms
without a click. Clicks reach the handlers whichever movements `interactions`
turns off.
